Volume and jitter copies

During training, the model sees every real window plus one changed copy of it. The copy gets two changes: volume makes the whole face quieter or louder, and jitter changes every single value a little. All windows on this page are real training windows.

Part 1 · The short version

Three steps for one copy

step 1 · one numberVolume The whole window is multiplied by one random number. The face gets quieter or louder, but keeps its shape.
step 2 · 350 numbersJitter Every value (5 frames × 70 action units) is multiplied by its own random number between 0.8 and 1.2.
step 3 · safety rulesClip and check Values stay between 0 and 1. A Neutral copy is scaled down if any value goes above 0.25.

16,362 windows per epoch, shuffled together. The copies are made again with new random numbers at the start of every epoch and are never saved. The real windows are never changed. A copy keeps the label and the sample weight of its original.

Short answer: volume + jitter is the best model when the player is not calibrated (0.616 against 0.584, better in 8 of 10 sessions). With movement calibration every model gives the same result (0.737 to 0.741), so the copies no longer matter there. Only one seed was trained, and the gain of jitter on top of volume (about +0.01) is smaller than the seed noise (about 0.02).

Part 2 · The details
The exact rule

How the numbers are drawn

StepRuleWhy
Face sizemean over the 5 frames of the sum of the 63 face action unitstells how strong this face is
Volume rangefrom 0.8 ÷ size to 5.5 ÷ size, each kept inside ×1/3 … ×3aims the copy at a face size between 0.8 (quiet headset faces) and 5.5 (strong faces)
Volume numberrandom, log-uniform in that range (×0.5 and ×2 are equally likely)quieter and louder copies are balanced
Neutral volume×0.5 … ×1.5, not aimed at a sizea resting face must stay a resting face
Jitter numbersone per action unit per frame, uniform 0.8 … 1.2 (mean 1.0)small changes of shape and timing; zero stays zero, so no new movement appears
Clipevery value kept in 0 … 1the headset never reports more than 1
Neutral checkif the largest value of a Neutral copy is above 0.25, the whole copy is scaled down to 0.25the same limit model C used
Measured on all 8,181 training windows

What each method does to the windows

How to read: shape similarity is the cosine between a copy and its original (1.000 = the same shape). Size ratio is copy size ÷ original size for expressive windows: below 1 = quieter, above 1 = louder. For comparison, the same person on different days has a shape similarity of 0.88, and two different people 0.50.

The median volume copy is quieter (×0.74) because most training windows are strong EmojiHero faces, which are pulled down toward headset strength. Quiet windows are pulled up.

Limits

One seed per model. The headset sessions are mostly one person. Jitter changes the shape only a little (0.993), much less than the difference between people (0.50), so it cannot teach the model how other people look.
